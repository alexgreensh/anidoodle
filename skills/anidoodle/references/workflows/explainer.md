# Explainers and infographics

An explainer teaches one idea per chapter; an infographic makes numbers readable at a glance.
Both are judged on whether a viewer understands something true afterwards, so the writing and
the facts come before any drawing.

## The research ledger (before the storyboard)

For every claim the piece makes: the claim in one sentence, the source (URL or exact passage),
your confidence, and what you are simplifying. A claim you cannot source is cut or clearly shown
as a metaphor. Numbers on screen come from the ledger, and each carries its source in a comment
beside the code that draws it. Record the audience (age band, expertise) because it sets the
reading level and the pace.

## The storyboard (`STORYBOARD.md`)

One row per chapter or shot: claim id, the one-sentence takeaway, the on-screen caption, the
visual metaphor and where it stops being literal, what the recurring character or token does,
the frame range on the beat grid, the music and sound cues, the transition out. Show it for
review when the person wants that step; otherwise build from it.

## Picture rules

- **One concept on screen at a time.** Build it up in the order the viewer needs it, marks drawn
  on, never faded in. The drawing process (`drawing-process.md`) is the explainer's best tool:
  the diagram assembling IS the explanation.
- **Captions are drawn lettering** (`lettering.ts`, `drafting.ts`, a style's own hand), sized for
  the smallest shape the piece ships in. Keep them short enough to read twice in their time on
  screen.
- **A recurring guide** (a mascot, a hand, an object) gives continuity across chapters; build it
  once as a character module.
- **Charts are drawn to scale** from the ledger's numbers, labelled with the values they reach,
  with annotations in the style's hand (the scrapbook hand's red pencil marks, a blueprint's
  dimension lines).
- **Styles that suit explaining**: scrapbook (archival, research-y), blueprint (technical),
  chalkboard (a lesson), flat vector and isometric (products and systems), paper-craft
  (playful, for children), marker comic (energetic). Say why you chose one.

## Infographic stills

Same ledger, one page. A clear reading order (top-left in, one path through), one hero figure,
three to seven supporting facts, every number sourced. Ship it as a still at each shape the
person needs; a vertical version is a relayout, not a crop.

## Checks

- Every caption on screen long enough to read in the chosen audience's pace (a human pass).
- Every chapter owns a non-empty frame range; the chapters tile the film with no gaps.
- A phone-width check of the smallest shipped shape: no caption under a readable size.
- Delivery says which facts were independently checked and what was simplified.
