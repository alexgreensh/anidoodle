# Pop-up card (paper engineering) · `popUp.ts`

**Medium, physically.** One sheet of heavy cream card, folded once down the middle, laid on a kraft-paper table and opened flat. Glued to the right page are the pop-ups: scissor-cut pieces of coloured and printed paper, each hinged on a valley fold along its bottom edge, each with the white core a knife leaves and a darker cut edge. Reference, from memory: Robert Sabuda's and Matthew Reinhart's pop-up books (valley-fold layers, paper struts).

**The fold is the style.** A piece is drawn in its own plane and hinged about its crease: flat on the page it is foreshortened like the page, standing it faces the viewer. The projection is one line: picture height = d * sin(elevation + fold), about the crease, with the view's elevation constant. No 3D engine, no perspective solve — the hinge and the tone do the work.

**The marks.** Flat colour and printed motifs (dot prints, confetti, a printed rule border), never a drawn line. Three tones tell every fold: the face, lit a little more as it stands toward the sun; a soft valley shadow laid in the crease, deepening as the piece rises; and the piece's own shadow on the page, sliding away down-right and softening as it lifts. The centre fold keeps its gutter shadow; the swinging cover's shadow tracks it across the table.

**The edge.** Scissor-crisp, then the white core of the cut (a round-join stroke, exactly the margin scissors leave), then the card's own darker cut edge down-right.

**Order of marks.** The table. The card's shadow. The cover, swinging open around the spine; the card is recentred as it opens, the way a hand adjusts it. The right page printed. The crease valleys. Then the pop-ups back to front: two hills, five flowers, a butterfly on a paper strut, a grass fringe. The red heart on the cover returns printed on the inside page and at the middle bloom.

**Palette.** Cream card on kraft; coral, butter, rose and one true red for the blooms; sage and deep green for hills and grass; a lilac butterfly. Sun: upper left, so every cut edge and shadow falls down-right, as in `paperCraft`'s world.

**Not its neighbours.** `paperCraft` is a toy theatre: flats slid into a stage gap, stop-motion on twos. `fox` is collage with no space between layers. This is one sheet folded: everything hinged, everything's depth from the fold angle, the motion a glide and a paper spring, never a held pose.

**Motion grammar.** The cover swings on an eased 0-to-180 arc. Each fold waits for the cover to pass, then rises with a damped spring overshoot, back rows first; standing paper keeps a small sway, and the butterfly opens one flutter. Nothing fades in; pieces are always there, folded flat.
